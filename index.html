Before You Switch

The questions everyone asks first.

How do I know if an alert is real or a false alarm?

Photo hits your phone in 9 seconds. Cat. Ignore. Person. Call response. No guessing.

My current alarm already has a SIM card. What makes Ajax different?

A single SIM card is one channel. If that carrier goes down, runs out of credit, or someone uses a cheap GSM jammer, the system goes silent. Ajax runs Ethernet and two SIM cards from two different networks simultaneously. To silence Ajax you would need to cut the Ethernet cable and jam two separate carrier networks at the same time. If jamming is attempted at the wireless sensor frequency, Ajax detects it and sends a jamming alert to your monitoring centre via cellular, which runs on a completely different frequency. One channel is a vulnerability. Three running in parallel are not.

What happens during a KPLC blackout?

Every device carries its own battery. A standard wired panel runs on a 7Ah battery that lasts roughly 8 hours before it shuts down. The Ajax hub keeps running and transmitting for up to 16 hours. When KPLC restores power, it recharges automatically.

Is switching from a wired system a big, messy job?

No drilling. No trunking. No chasing cable through concrete. Sensors clip or screw to the surface and talk wirelessly to the hub. A typical Nairobi home is fully installed and tested in one day.

How long before sensors need replacing?

Standard door and motion sensors run 5 to 7 years on a single battery. The MotionCam runs 3 to 4 years. The system monitors its own battery levels and alerts you in the app six months before any device actually needs attention, so there are no emergency callouts and no gaps in protection.

Could my current setup already have blind spots I don't know about?

Yes, and most people don't find out until something happens. Wired sensors fail silently from oxidation on terminal connections. The panel shows "armed" but the sensor stopped responding months ago. The free site assessment checks this before you spend a shilling on anything new.
